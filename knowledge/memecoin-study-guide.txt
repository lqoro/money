1. The big picture: principles to remember
• The game favors people with information (influencers, devs, launchpads, market makers, insider

groups). Counter it by tracking the wallets of people with information, not by copying them (V5).
• Never become exit liquidity. Do not copy-trade wallets, and do not buy just because a big wallet or

a big account bought or tweeted. They can sell on top of you (V1, V4, V9, V18).
• Plan the exit before the entry. Most new pairs never bond; assume a typical new pair dies around

30-40k market cap and sell by then unless you are very confident it will bond (V1).
• Be early to the meta but not late. Metas now last hours to days; copycats and knockoffs rarely beat

the original (V1, V5).
• Trade only when everything lines up. Do not force trades (V6).
• Trade with a community and use different perspectives, but beware Telegram scammers: no

creator or mod will ever DM you first (V3, V5, V7).
• Treat losses as tuition, and size for survival. Memecoins are the high-risk bucket of a portfolio, not

the whole portfolio (V8).

3. Hunting workflow and filters
• Axiom Pulse, New Pairs: protocols = Pump.fun only (optionally Bonk/Mayhem). Metrics: minimum

market cap about 10% of the bond (migration) price, e.g. 5,100 when bond is 51k, 6,000 when
bond is 60k, about 3,500 when bond is about 34k. The bond estimate is shown at the bottom of
Axiom (V1, V2, V17).
• Final Stretch: same protocol filter. Want a clean organic chart, a good idea and about $10k volume

per 5 minutes ($2k is "fine but not optimal"; $500 is too little) (V1).
• Dex Screener tab (migrated coins, lower risk): 5-minute timeframe, age max 12 hours, minimum

market cap about $30k, sort by volume (V1).
• Dex Screener recon (V6): min market cap $70k, max about $11M, 5-minute volume at least $10k,

boosted filter on. Boost sweet spot is about 200-1,000 (500+ gives a golden ticker); 3,500-4,000
boosts (about $20,000 spent) is suspicious.
• Read the ticker: you are trading memes. The idea must be funny and make sense to a wide

audience (V6).
• Fits the meta, but is an original idea around the meta (not a knockoff). Example: "I like turtles"

during the Franklin the turtle meta (V1).
• Vamps: copycats that drain liquidity from the original. Use Similar Tokens to find the original; in a

vamp situation look in Final Stretch for the one breaking through (V1, V2, V17).

5. Thresholds that differ by video (know which context)
• New Pairs minimum market cap: about 10% of bond (V1, V2, V17). Dex Screener recon

minimum: $70k (V6). Dex tab on Axiom: $30k (V1). CTO candidates: graduated tokens at or
below $6,000 market cap (V9).
• Bundles: under 10% bundled is fine; 30-80% do not buy (V1). Single bundle over 3-4% is suspicious

(V3). Bundlers under 10% (V13).
• Top 10 holders: 15% max (V6), under 25% (V13), 30% or more is bad on EVM chains (V10).
• Volume per 5 minutes: at least $10k (V1, V6, V13); $500 too low (V1); $300 is a red flag (V1).
• Wallet to track: win rate at least about 40% (50% is really good), 7-day transactions of at least 10,

many different trades (V4).
• Pre-pump buyer hunt on GMGN: buys only, amount over $500 (V4).
• CTO holders: each wallet under about 2% of supply (3.85% is risky) (V9).
• Slippage: buy about 5-10%, sell about the same or maybe 20%; buy slippage lower than sell

slippage (V17).
• Dev holdings: 0% to under 1% (V13, V17).

• Never trade Solana memecoins from a Phantom wallet: bots can front-run (MEV attack). Use

always.

instead of 0.001 for priority and bribe.

calls it scummy.

7. Scams and security
• Airdropped tokens: do not touch; hide in Phantom via manage token list (V3).
• Fake support / "wallet unverified" emails, fake "Kick token" sites, fake trading bots, fake

Safeguard bots, impersonation DMs, "VIP group" offers: never send SOL or crypto to someone
to trade for you; never share a seed phrase on a call (V7).
• Lookalike news domains: check domain age (V3).
• Googling crypto sites gives scam ads: use official links or CoinMarketCap (V4, V8, V12).
• Wrong chain / mistyped address = permanent loss; no on-chain support (V12). Store the

12-word recovery phrase offline (V12).
• Exchange security (Kraken Pro, V14): 2FA, funding 2FA, master key stored offline, global settings

lock. Instant buy is a flat 1% fee; Pro has maker/taker fees. Withdrawal example: 0.005 SOL fee,
0.011 SOL minimum.
• Prop firms (V15): Solana Funded accounts were all demo, terms allow refusing any payout,

founders not doxed, brand new.
• Casinos (V16): every house game has negative expected value for the player; winners are

advertised, losers are not.

Age 65 retired on pension: 100% Bitcoin/majors. Age 18, $500k/yr, $5k: risk 10: 5% majors, 15%
metas, 80% memes.

07. How to Stay Safe from Crypto Hackers
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Vm6S_Q3Hxw

TL;DR
Scammers exploit primal instincts (getting rich, security, rare opportunity, sex, fear) and create urgency,

Key Concepts & Rules
• Know the scammer profile [01:05]
• They target instincts: get rich, have security, find a rare opportunity, sex, and fear.
• They create fear and urgency, often with an unrealistic opportunity ("you found a gem, act now").
• They use forgery/impersonation to piggyback on trusted brands and people; impersonation

creates two victims (the person attacked and the brand/person impersonated).
• You do not always need to interact with a scammer: many memecoin, utility or alt tokens are

scams in themselves, with developers extracting money through the token.
• The "Kick token" scam [02:45]
• Seen on new-pairs platforms (Nova, Axiom, BullX-type platforms) as new tokens with x-rated

images as the icon (example token named "strip coin").
• The token's link sends you to a fake version of the live-streaming site Kick. Interacting with its

"verification" panel installs a Trojan virus.
• The Trojan gives the hacker full access to your browser and the wallets in it, and can hack your

Telegram too (many wallets are hosted on Telegram), so you get drained.
• The scammer makes money from the Trojan, not from rugging the token.
• Fake support / fear emails [04:25]
• Example: spam email claiming to be Trust Wallet saying your wallet is "unverified" and will be

suspended, with a verify button.
• Scammers shotgun every user they can reach (the Coinbase-user scams cost victims millions);

eventually they hit someone who really has that account.
• The goal is a fake link or a call with a fake support agent who asks for your seed phrase.
• Rule: be very skeptical of anyone who contacts you in crypto.
• Impersonation of creators [06:05]

Page 37

• Vic's Telegram channel (almost 30,000 people) has "I'll never DM you" in the name; the group

chat (almost 10,000) has an auto-reply when you type "reminder": he will never DM you or sell a

• A scammer always reaches out first. A DM from someone you do not know, or that does not make

sense, is a red flag.
• Fake Telegram trading bots [07:46]
• Someone DMs you recommending an unknown bot, or a known bot with a different username.

Code for fake bots is cheap and accessible on the scammer black market.
• They are wallet drainers: giving permissions to your Telegram gets you drained, or they tell you to

• Safeguard is a real verification bot you see when joining trusted channels. Verify it is literally

• Scammers make knock-off accounts with the same image and name and install them in token

• Secure your accounts [10:01]
• Put two-factor authentication on Telegram and on your email (Gmail), using Google Authenticator,

• Exit liquidity (the indirect scam) [10:35]

market cap, tell the world, it goes to $500,000; the money that came in is your exit liquidity.
• P&L cards (e.g. Axiom's) can be hijacked/altered, and people print fake massive returns. Posting a

big P&L is step one of gathering followers for exit liquidity.
• Scammer bios often link a call channel: they create tokens, give the community early access, then

farm them. Free tier gets the contract address late; paid tier (5 or 10 SOL) gets calls early, and the
paid tier uses the free tier as exit liquidity if the community grows.
• DM variant: "send me Solana and I'll trade it and return profit". People send 3 or 5 SOL, get

blocked and ghosted.
• Mechanisms behind these farms: sniping, bundling and liquidity farming; the scammer puts in own

money to lift the chart and takes money out as more comes in.

delay lets viewers buy right after him and become his exit liquidity).

Page 38

• On-chain casinos have super low fees and no overhead for a facility or staff, so millions or
