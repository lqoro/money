## 84. Stanford's Method Turns Claude Into a PHD Level Research Team
- **Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tj3018n5MVg | **Length:** 12 min | **AI-relevant:** yes
- **Teaches:**
  - Adapts Stanford's STORM research method into a Claude skill: five perspective agents (practitioner, academic, skeptic, economist, historian) each research one topic.
  - Chains four prompts: spin up the perspectives, map where they contradict each other, synthesize one HTML briefing, then peer-review it and verify each citation (confirmed, corrected, or demoted).
  - Output starts with a 60-second summary and key findings ranked by reliability, and names missing lenses (here, the customer and frontline employee).
  - Compares its output with Claude Code's built-in deep research; Codex, run as a judge, preferred the STORM briefing on six criteria (Nate's account).
  - Separates subagents (report only to the main session) from agent teams (can debate each other, but cost more).
- **Tools / prices / numbers:**
  - Claude Code deep research: his run used about 103 agents and hit API rate limits.
  - STORM run: about 12 agents, which he says was faster and cheaper.
  - Stanford claim he cites: articles 25 percent more organized in peer-reviewed testing.
  - Subagents ran on Opus 4.8; Haiku or Sonnet are options.
  - Skill installs in the .claude folder; other agents use their own folders (.codex or .agents).
- **Skills shown:** skill authoring (SKILL.md plus an HTML template), multi-agent orchestration, citation verification, research prompting, cross-model comparison.
- **Money angle:** none stated. He suggests tailoring the skill to your business and goals, and using it to pick topics for content.
- **Duet-relevant:** no
- **Promo:** the skill and HTML template are free in his school community, under the classroom's YouTube resources section.
